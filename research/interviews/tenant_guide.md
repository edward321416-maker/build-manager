# Tenant interview guide

Status: **[DECISION] interview design**, not conducted research. Target tenants in studios, officetels, and multifamily rental housing. Recruitment, consent language, sample size, and schedule are **[TO VERIFY]**. Do not recruit or contact participants without authorization.

Explain voluntary participation and the right to skip questions. Request no exact address, unit, landlord identity, contract, private message export, or payment information. Recording and public quotation require separate consent. Keep participant mappings and source records private under [research rules](../README.md).

## Neutral recent-event questions

1. Describe the last housing issue you reported, from noticing it to receiving a response.
2. What words or media did you use? What was difficult or easy to explain?
3. What did the landlord/manager ask next? What could you safely provide?
4. How did you know the report had been received, assigned, or resolved?
5. Was there a time you chose not to report a problem? What influenced that choice?
6. Describe a report that worked well through your existing channel.
7. What would you be uncomfortable photographing or sharing? What controls would you need?
8. In a situation you considered urgent, whom did you contact and what information was unclear? Do not reenact hazards or ask for risky tests.
9. If willing, walk through a clearly fictional reporting example. Keep observed usability behavior separate from opinions and real incidents.

## Analysis and follow-up

Test CLM-004 without assuming confusion or dissatisfaction. Distinguish actual recalled events, hypothetical preferences, and researcher interpretation. Record contradictions and selection/recall bias. Never infer all tenants behave alike from a few interviews. Public output contains anonymized patterns and limits, with claim IDs and no identifying narrative. An active safety concern ends the interview task and triggers the product's human safety escalation boundary.
