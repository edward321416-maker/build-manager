# Landlord / manager interview guide

Status: **[DECISION] interview design**, not conducted research. Recruit landlords around the target 10–100-unit range and small/medium managers; verify fit privately and include contrasting cases. Sample size, channels, schedule, and recruitment permission are **[TO VERIFY]**. Do not contact anyone without authorization.

## Consent and framing

Explain that this is problem research, participation is voluntary, questions may be skipped, and no legal/technical diagnosis is provided. Obtain separate permission for recording and any quote use. Do not request tenant names, addresses, contracts, or message exports. Use a private participant code; follow [research privacy rules](../README.md).

## Recent-event questions

1. Think of the most recent maintenance report you handled. What happened from first contact to closure?
2. What did the tenant initially send? Which information was missing, if any?
3. How did you decide the next action and who should handle it? What uncertainty remained?
4. Which handoffs, follow-up messages, scheduling steps, or repeated visits occurred? Ask for remembered events; mark estimates as estimates.
5. How is the result recorded and retrieved when the same unit reports another problem?
6. Describe a case that went smoothly. What made the existing method sufficient?
7. Have you abandoned a tool or process? Why? Who decides whether a new tool is adopted?
8. What information would you refuse to share with an AI service? When must a human take over?
9. Which unresolved task matters most? What alternative do you use today?

Do not pitch savings, suggest an expected pain score, or ask a leading willingness-to-pay question. Separate later concept/pricing research from observed problem evidence. Record contrary evidence and no-problem cases.

## Analysis and exit criteria

Capture process steps, anonymous role/context, reported events, uncertainty, current alternatives, and follow-up consent in private notes. Create public aggregate paraphrases only after privacy review. Link evidence to CLM-003 and CLM-007, distinguishing testimony from observed artifacts. Recruitment and segment saturation criteria must be defined before claiming representativeness. Output is an evidence note with limitations, not a fabricated interview result.
